# Conventions and fixed illustrative values

All parameters are chosen ideal-model values, not measured. Angular velocities omega are in rad/s; nu=omega/(2 pi) is in rev/s. Angles are oriented about +z. All signed powers are positive into the boundary named beside them. Internal mesh and pin sides are paired diagnostics, never extra external inputs.

In the opening geometric bound, mathsf H is a positive-definite energy matrix and mathsf O is the linear observation map. Neither is the scalar mechanical angular momentum H. The engineering decision limits are bW=1/5000000 J+(1/500000) sum abs(Wj) and bPsi=1/50000000 V s+(1/500000) sum abs(Psil); these are illustrative criteria, not certified uncertainty bounds.

## Active reference and limiting models

The active capacitor voltage is w=VA-VR=alpha(omega-Omega). Id=tau/alpha and Ie=-C dot(VR) are separate floating source currents from R into A. This represents relative kinetic energy, without the effective centrifugal potential. The reference-only control uses J=1 kg m^2, alpha=1 V/(rad/s), C=1 F, tau=0, Omega=2t rad/s^2, and zero initial states on [0,1] s. Reference work is +2 J. Driver Cd=1/200 F and Rd=3/4 ohm give command work 403/40000 J, heat -3/40000 J, and final store 1/100 J. Disabled compensation gives a -2 J electrical-minus-mechanical store difference with both separate balances closed.

The active compound topology restores all four node capacitors and their physical returns at R, including the held-member capacitor. Four node actuators implement the original current commands, and four compensators inject -CX dot(r). These are ideal regenerative converter outputs; original source/load coefficients define current commands, not additional heat paths. Four winding resistors remain physical. The additional reference driver uses Cd=1/200 F and Rd=3/4 ohm; it does not replace the original carrier capacitor. Nine ideal converters share a Cs=1 F rail initialized at 100 V with controller Gq=1/1000 S. Positive rail voltage is an explicit condition, not a claimed general result. Rail preparation on [-1,0] s uses 100 A through 1 ohm: source work 15000 J, heat -10000 J, final store 5000 J. A 1 V reference change over the three transition widths gives driver heat (-3/160,-3/80,-3/40) J and command work (17/800,1/25,31/400) J, with store increase 1/400 J.

The joint rigid limit uses 0<epsilon<=1, L0=3/(25 epsilon) H, k=1-epsilon^2, resistance coefficient 2 epsilon^2/25 ohm, and all node capacitances/inertias scaled by epsilon. All four node voltages are clamped to the rigid rates times alpha, with initial ideal currents. Modal sums s=h i1+i2 start at zero, differences d=h i1-i2 start at 2h i1*. All operation works on [0,3/2] s have exponential primitives. Separate linear preparation ramps on [-1/10,0] s include each winding source, heat export, and capacitor source. Fixed nonzero modal-sum initial data are excluded from the convergence result.

Singular controls retain descriptor KCL/KVL rather than invert a zero capacitance or rank-one inductance. At k=1 each positive-resistance pair retains phi=L0(h i1+i2), energy phi^2/(2L0), and algebraic current constraints. Zero source resistance is an imposed voltage with a separately solved reaction current. Clamp events use the limit of a finite resistance law, integrating source and heat before taking the limit. Finite clamp conductance is 20C/delta; an isolated capacitor retains voltage error (vminus-U)exp(-20) at the end of that finite interval.

Finite reversal controls set winding resistance to zero, retain finite capacitances/inductances, and prescribe node voltages u f(t/T*) on [0,T*], T*=1 s. Profiles are f1=z(1-z)(1-2z), f2=z(1-z)(z-1/3)^2(z-5/7), and zero. Their primitives are z^2(1-z)^2/2 and -z^2(1-z)^2(21z^2-18z+5)/126. For k>0, uH=uP=0,uC=1 V,uF=(1+hF/hH-2hF/k) V; at k=0, uC=uP=uH=0,uF=1 V. Both held members, five geometries plus the two-external equal-ratio assembly, k={0,19/20,99/100}, and all four frames are covered by the algebraic proof. All individual signed full-interval works are zero; integrated absolute power is a different observable. Exact root completeness applies only to the polynomial histories.
